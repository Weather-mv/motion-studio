# Style notes from the references
Style only. No text, branding, characters or footage from either file may be used.
Method: 1 frame per second contact sheets plus ffmpeg scene-change detection (threshold 0.3). Motion between sampled frames is inferred, not measured.

## ref.mp4 (93 s, 1920x1080, 30 fps): kinetic type over a light gradient
- Pace: new idea about every 2 to 4 s. Only 1 hard cut detected in 93 s, so scenes change through continuous moves.
- Type: rounded sans, 1 to 5 words on screen, two-tone with a key word in an accent colour. Very large words are used occasionally for emphasis.
- Type motion: letters fly or scatter in and resolve, or words track in with wide spacing. Text then often slides up to make room for the visual below.
- Transitions: zooms into UI, the logo scaling through the camera, and text passing to a visual on the same background.
- UI: a screen in a thin rounded frame, pushed in on the part being discussed, with a cursor.
- End: logo, then tagline, then a URL pill with the action button. Roughly 8 s, which this job cannot afford.

## ref-2.mp4 (25.4 s, 1920x1080, 29.97 fps): cinematic, same product family
- Pace: 8 cuts detected in 25.4 s, mostly 1.3 to 2.7 s apart at the start, then longer holds.
- Mood: a dimmed, dark problem section releases into a bright branded gradient. This is the closest model for night site to light dashboard.
- Transitions: blur dissolves into and out of the logo, plus hard cuts.
- UI: checklist rows tick on in sequence, with one or two words beside them changing in step.
- End: logo, then two-tone lines one at a time, then a pill button with a cursor. Centred, which the V.E.R.O.N. guideline does not allow.

## What to carry over
- One idea at a time, with captions landing on the spoken word.
- Dark to light as the problem-to-solution turn.
- Rows or fields lighting in sequence as they are named.
- Soft blur and push-in moves rather than effects.

## What not to carry over
- Scattered or illegible letter animation, because the guideline voice is precise and calm.
- Centred layouts, because the guideline says left-align everything.
- Orange as a general accent, because orange is for alerts only here.
- Illustrated cartoon characters or stock-style people, because the guideline bans clip-art and stock photos.
